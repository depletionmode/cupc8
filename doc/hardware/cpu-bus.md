# CPU bus and CPU socket (Milestone 1)

The CPU lives on a plug-in **CPU card**. The main board's **chipset** FPGA
answers every CPU bus cycle: from SRAM, from the ROM windows, or from its own
I/O registers (see `memory-map.md`). The CPU card never drives the memory bus.

This bus is the contract between them. The M1 FPGA CPU card and a future
discrete-logic CPU card must both pass the same conformance testbench
(`soc/tb/cpubus_conformance`) against this document.

## Electrical

- **Signalling:** 3.3 V LVCMOS in both directions. The inputs on both sides
  are **not** 5 V tolerant. A 5 V discrete card must level-shift on its own
  side, for example with 74LVC245/74LVC1T45.
- **Pull-ups (main board):** /STB, /CPU_RST, PRSNT2_n and CARD_ID[1:0] have
  10 kΩ to 3V3. So an empty socket reads as "no cycle, no card".
- **Weak keepers:** D[7:0] have a 47 kΩ pull-up on the main board, so a
  floating bus reads `$FF`.
- **Series termination:** every card output has 33 Ω at the card driver, and
  every chipset output has 33 Ω at the chipset.

## Clocking

- **CPU_CLK** is generated by the chipset and is the only clock the card may
  use. Both sides sample on its **rising edge**.
- **Frequency:** fixed at 12 MHz in M1. The divider bits in `CPU_CTL` are
  reserved and must be 0. A slower card (such as the M2 discrete CPU) is
  served by /RDY wait states and stop/step, which need no clock change.
- **Stopping:** CPU_CLK never stops while /CPU_RST is high. Stopping and
  stepping are done with /RDY, not by gating the clock.

## Signals

| Signal | Driver | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| CPU_CLK | chipset | Bus clock |
| /CPU_RST | chipset | Active-low reset. It is asserted for ≥ 16 CPU_CLK edges at power-up, by sysctl, and by the reset button. While it is low the card must drive /STB high and must not drive D. |
| A[15:0] | card | Address, valid while /STB is low |
| RW | card | 1 = read, 0 = write, valid while /STB is low |
| D[7:0] | both | Data. The card drives it only during write cycles. The chipset drives it only during read cycles, and only in the clock where it asserts /RDY. |
| /STB | card | Cycle request, active low |
| /RDY | chipset | Cycle completion, active low, one clock wide |
| SYNC | card | High during an opcode-fetch cycle: the first byte of an instruction. Not asserted for operand, stack or vector cycles. |
| IRQ[3:0] | chipset | `IRQ_PEND & IRQ_MASK` as levels. The CPU samples them only at its instruction boundary. |
| TMR_EXP[1:0] | card | A one-clock high pulse when timer 0/1 crosses to zero. The chipset latches it into IRQ_PEND bits 1/2. |
| HALTED | card | High after `HALT` executes, until reset |
| WAITING | card | High while `WAI` is waiting for an IRQ |
| CRESET_n, CDONE, CFG_SCK, CFG_MOSI, CFG_SS_n | sysctl ↔ card | FPGA-card configuration only. A card without an FPGA leaves these unconnected. |
| CARD_ID[1:0] | card | Card type, set by straps to GND: `11` = none (pull-ups), `10` = FPGA CPU card, `01` = discrete CPU card, `00` = reserved |
| PRSNT1_n / PRSNT2_n | card | A1 is GND on the main board. The card joins A1 to B49 (PRSNT2_n). The main board sees PRSNT2_n low only when the card is fully seated. |

## Bus cycle

The protocol is a single-master classic handshake, like Wishbone classic with
CYC and STB merged.

- **Request.** At rising edge **n**, the card has put a stable A, RW, SYNC
  (and D for writes) on the bus, with /STB low.
- **Wait states.** The chipset samples /STB low at edge **n**. It asserts /RDY
  low for exactly one clock at some later edge **m ≥ n+1**. Each clock
  between n and m is a wait state, and there can be any number of them.
- **Read data.** In a read, the chipset drives D starting from the same edge
  where /RDY goes low. D is valid at edge **m+1**, when the card samples /RDY
  low and latches D. The chipset stops driving D at edge **m+1**.
- **Write data.** In a write, the card holds D stable from before edge **n**
  until edge **m+1**.
- **End of cycle.** At edge **m+1** the cycle is over. The card may either:
  - raise /STB (idle), or
  - keep /STB low and present a new address, which starts a new cycle that
    the chipset samples at edge **m+1**.

```
edge:       n      n+1     m      m+1
CPU_CLK  _/‾\_/‾\_/‾\_/‾\_/‾\_/‾\_/‾\_
/STB     ‾‾\_________________________/‾   (or stays low: next cycle)
A,RW     ==X=========================X=
/RDY     ‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾\_______/‾‾‾‾‾‾   one clock wide
D (read) --------------<=======>-------   chipset drives m..m+1
```

**Minimum cycle:** 2 clocks, because /RDY cannot be asserted until the edge
after /STB is sampled. I/O register cycles take 2 clocks.

**SRAM and ROM cycles take 4 clocks** (2 wait states):
1. At the sampling edge, the chipset drives the memory address and chip
   select.
2. The memory then has two full clocks (166 ns) before the chipset registers
   its data. That covers the 70 ns ROM and 45 ns SRAM plus the I/O delays.
3. /RDY is asserted with the registered data.

Writes pulse /WE for one clock (83 ns > tWP), in the middle of the address
window.

**Bus turnaround rule:** the chipset never drives D in the first clock of a
cycle. Every write→read transition therefore has at least one clock with
nobody driving D.

## Instruction boundary, interrupts and timers

- **IRQs.** The CPU samples IRQ[3:0] only between instructions, as it does
  today in `cpu.vhd` `check_irq`. It takes the lowest-numbered one.
- **IRQ entry.** Entry is the push/vector sequence from the manual: push pch,
  push pcl, push f, clear I, read the vector at $0010+2n. Every one of those is
  a normal bus cycle with SYNC low.
- **Timers.** They live on the CPU card, because `TMR0`/`TMR1` are
  instructions. They decrement once per retired instruction, including WAI
  idle iterations, exactly as `cpu.vhd` and `sim.nim` do today.
  - Each timer's `TMR_EXP` pulse goes to the chipset.
  - The chipset latches IRQ_PEND on the rising edge of that pulse.

## Stop, step and trace (chipset + sysctl)

The chipset controls /RDY, so it can control the CPU without the card's
cooperation.

- **Stop.** The chipset stops issuing /RDY. The CPU stalls in whatever cycle
  it is in.
- **Step (instruction).** The chipset completes cycles until it sees the next
  cycle with SYNC high. It holds that cycle un-acknowledged, which leaves the
  CPU stopped at the start of the next instruction.
- **Step (bus cycle).** The chipset completes exactly one cycle.
- **Trace.** Every completed cycle `{A, D, RW, SYNC}` goes into a 512-entry
  BRAM ring. sysctl reads it through the bus bridge. `cupc8.py trace`
  disassembles the SYNC cycles and can diff them against `sim.nim --trace`.
- **While the CPU is stopped,** the bus bridge may use the memory bus. When it
  releases, the chipset resumes by completing the stalled CPU cycle with the
  bus in its normal state. The CPU cannot tell the difference.

Bridge commands, in addition to those in `memory-map.md`. CPU state is read with `$05 STATUS`:

| Cmd | Bytes after cmd | Action |
|---|---|---|
| $07 CPU_CTL | ctl8 | bit0 stop, bit1 step-instruction, bit2 step-cycle, bits 5:3 reserved (0), bit6 assert /CPU_RST |
| $08 TRACE_RD | → count16, then count × {A16, D8, flags8} | Drain the trace ring. flags: bit0 RW, bit1 SYNC, bit7 overflowed since last read. |

## Timing budget (12 MHz, 83.3 ns period)

Worst-case numbers are verified by `hw/timing/cpubus_budget.py` against the
real FPGA I/O timings (icetime/nextpnr), the connector datasheet and the
extracted trace lengths. Required margin: ≥ 30% of the period. Initial
estimate for card → chipset:

| Item | ns |
|---|---|
| Card clock-to-out (iCE40 HX, LVCMOS33) | 8 |
| 33 Ω + trace + connector, ~80 mm total | 1.5 |
| Clock skew between CPU_CLK at the card and at the chipset | 2 |
| Chipset input setup | 3 |
| **Total** | **14.5 → 68.8 ns slack (83%)** |

The same order applies chipset → card. The slow path is the SRAM/ROM access
inside the chipset, which is covered by the wait state rather than by this
budget.

## CPU socket: PCIe x8 card edge

- **Connector:** a 98-contact PCIe x8 vertical socket, 1.0 mm pitch, on the
  main board. The CPU card is a 1.6 mm PCB with 49 gold fingers per side and a
  standard PCIe x8 key.
- **Custom pinout:** it does **not** carry PCIe signalling. Never plug a real
  PCIe card into it. The +5V pins would meet PCIe 12V/3.3V pins wired
  differently.
- **Pin budget:**
  - 5× +5V and 3× +3V3, about 1 A per contact.
  - 34× GND. Every bus signal has a GND on at least one neighbour, and
    CPU_CLK has GND on both neighbours and opposite.
  - 10 reserved pins for M2 (for example microcode-step debug on a discrete
    card).
- **Unused pins:** RSVD pins are left unconnected on both sides. The main
  board routes them to test pads.

## Conformance testbench (acceptance test for any CPU card)

`soc/tb/cpubus_conformance` drives the card through the socket signals only.
A card passes when all of the following hold.

1. **Protocol monitor:** zero violations. /STB, A, RW and write data are held
   stable until /RDY. D is not driven outside the rules above. Nothing is
   driven during /CPU_RST.
2. **Lockstep:** the completed-cycle trace equals `sim.nim`'s for every
   program in `test/`, `tools/testdata/` and the random-program fuzzer (≥ 10⁶
   instructions). This holds under:
   - 0 wait states, fixed 1–7 wait states, and random 0–15 wait states
   - random stop/step sequences
   - IRQs raised at every clock phase relative to the instruction boundary
   - reset asserted at random points (the CPU must restart cleanly at $e000)
3. **Timing:** at 12 MHz, with the chipset model sampling at ±30% of the
   setup/hold margins.
